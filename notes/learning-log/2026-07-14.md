## 🔭 Observability → [full note](../observability.md)

**The core distinction:** *monitoring* handles **known unknowns** (you predicted the failure, so you built the dashboard). *Observability* handles **unknown unknowns** — the ability to ask **new questions without shipping new code**. Monitoring says *that* something broke; observability tells you *why*.

What actually stuck:

- **The "three pillars" is a starter model, not the truth.** Metrics/logs/traces aren't separate foundations — they're three *views over the same events*. The better framing is **wide, structured events**: emit one rich event per unit of work, derive the rest. That's what makes after-the-fact questions answerable.
- **Cardinality is the constraint that explains everything.** High-cardinality fields (`user_id`, `build_id`) are exactly what you need to debug, but putting them in metric labels explodes your backend. Resolution: keep **metrics low-cardinality for alerting**, push the high-cardinality detail into **events/traces**.
- **Instrument by checklist, not vibes:** **RED** (Rate, Errors, Duration) for services; **USE** (Utilization, Saturation, Errors) for resources; **Four Golden Signals** (latency, traffic, errors, saturation) from the SRE book. And measure success vs. failure latency *separately* — fast failures flatter your graphs.
- **Percentiles, never averages.** A 200 ms mean can hide a p99 of 8 s, and the p99 is a real person.
- **Error budgets turn reliability into a currency.** `100% − SLO`; 99.9% ≈ 43 min/month of budget. Budget left → ship. Budget gone → freeze and fix. It replaces "how stable is stable enough?" arguments with a number. **100% is the wrong target** — infinitely expensive and users can't tell.
- **Correlation is the whole game:** `trace_id` on every log, exemplars linking metrics → traces. The debugging loop becomes *alert → exemplar trace → slow span → that span's logs*. Three hops to root cause.
- **Sample the boring, keep the interesting** — tail-based sampling keeps 100% of errors/slow requests and 1% of the dull ones. Head-based sampling throws away the rare error you actually wanted.

> One-liner to remember: *alert on symptoms users feel, debug with high-cardinality events — and never sample your errors away.*

## 🔗 Connections I noticed

- **"Read the traces" is the same instinct in two domains.** Loop engineering says every debugging insight comes from reading the raw agent transcript, not re-running experiments. Observability says the same about distributed systems: the trace *is* the evidence. Both are "stop guessing, go read what actually happened."
- **Error budget = the CRAP score of reliability.** Both take a fuzzy quality argument and turn it into a single number that dictates the action (refactor vs. test; freeze vs. ship). Good metrics don't just measure — they *decide*.
- **Cardinality is a read-vs-write trade-off again** (fifth sighting). Cheap aggregate metrics vs. expensive detailed events is the same shape as index-vs-write-cost and cache freshness-vs-speed. Pick the knob deliberately.
- **A dashboard is a cache of questions you already asked.** Fixed dashboards only answer what you anticipated — the staleness problem, applied to knowledge.
