## 🌐 Consistency Models & CAP → [full note](../consistency-models-cap.md)

**The core thesis:** the moment you keep more than one copy of data, the copies can disagree. A consistency model is the contract for *what a reader is guaranteed to see*. Stronger = easier to reason about but slower and less available; weaker = fast and available but complexity lands on you.

What actually stuck:

- **CAP is a forced choice *during a partition*, not a general "pick 2."** Partitions aren't optional (networks fail), so P is a given — the real question is: when nodes can't talk, do you stay **Consistent** (refuse/err) or **Available** (answer maybe-stale)? When the network is healthy you get both.
- **Two big misreadings I'd been carrying:** CAP's "C" = **linearizability**, *not* ACID's "C" (invariants). And "CA systems" basically don't exist in distributed computing.
- **PACELC is the honest framing:** if **P**artition → **A** or **C**; **E**lse → **L**atency or **C**onsistency. The consistency/latency bill is paid on *every request*, not just during failures. That's the trade-off I actually feel day to day.
- **The spectrum:** linearizable → sequential → causal → eventual. **Causal is the strongest model still available under a partition** — the sweet spot for AP systems, tracked with vector clocks. Eventual = "if writes stop, replicas converge eventually," and a **cache TTL is just eventual consistency with a staleness bound**.
- **Linearizability ≠ serializability — this was the big unlock.** Linearizability = single object, real-time recency (distributed systems). Serializability = multi-object transaction ordering (my isolation-levels note). Combine them = **strict serializability**. *My transactions note and this note are the same subject at different granularities.*
- **Quorums are the CAP dial made numeric:** `W + R > N` guarantees a read overlaps the latest write. `W=1,R=1` on N=3 → eventual + max availability. This is literally consistency-vs-availability as arithmetic.
- **Conflict resolution progression:** LWW (cheap, silently loses data, clock-dependent) → vector clocks (detect real concurrency) → **CRDTs** (merge deterministically with zero coordination). Same "design the problem away" instinct as immutability/idempotency.
- **Consistency is a per-operation decision, not per-system:** strong for balances/inventory, read-your-writes for profiles, eventual for likes/feeds/carts. Ask: *what does a stale read actually cost here?*

> One-liner to remember: *partitions force C-or-A, PACELC shows you also pay C-vs-latency every day, and the right consistency level is per-operation — pay for strong only where staleness is expensive.*

## 🔗 Connections I noticed — the month, unified

This is the note where the whole library clicked into one picture:

- **Everything I studied was one trade-off at different scales.** Isolation levels (transaction ordering), cache TTLs (staleness bound), at-least-once delivery (eventual convergence), and now consistency models — all local instances of **consistency vs. availability/latency**. I kept writing "the read-vs-write trade-off, Nth sighting"; *this* is its general form. It was the shape under all of them.

- **Read-your-writes explains my own caching rule.** "Invalidate/bypass the cache for the user who just wrote" — I'd written that as a tactic; it's a *named session guarantee*.
- **The papers in my index are now readable.** Dynamo (quorums, vector clocks), Spanner (TrueTime, external consistency = strict serializability), Lamport's clocks (causal ordering) — I collected them on day one without context; now they're the primary sources for today's note.
- **Idempotency (7th sighting) and CRDTs are cousins** — both make an operation safe to apply more than once / merge without coordination. Reliability keeps returning to "make repetition and disorder harmless."
